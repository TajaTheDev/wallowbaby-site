Before you start

5 safety basics for every recipe

1

Milk first until 12 months

Breast milk or infant formula stays your baby's main food until 12 months. Solids add to it. Weaning from the breast early? Ask your pediatrician which formula to use. No cow's milk as a drink and no juice before 12 months.

2

No honey before 12 months

Not in food, not baked in, not on a pacifier. Honey can carry the spores that cause infant botulism.

3

Match the texture to the stage

Smooth at 6 months, lumpy and soft at 8, small soft pieces at 10, chopped family food at 12+. If it doesn't squash between your fingers, it's not ready yet.

4

Know the choking hazards

Skip whole nuts, popcorn, hard raw veggies, whole corn kernels and hard candy. Quarter grapes and cherry tomatoes lengthwise. Thin nut butters, never a spoonful. Baby sits upright, and you stay close.

5

Introduce allergens early, one at a time

The AAP says there's no benefit to delaying allergens like egg, peanut, dairy, fish and sesame. Offer one new allergen at a time, at home, earlier in the day. Severe eczema or egg allergy? Talk to your pediatrician before peanut.

!

Gagging vs choking

Gagging is noisy and normal while babies learn. Choking is silent: no crying, coughing or breathing. Take an infant CPR class before starting solids.
