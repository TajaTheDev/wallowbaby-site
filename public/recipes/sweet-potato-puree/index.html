Silky sweet potato puree

Naturally sweet, bright orange and freezer-friendly: one of the easiest first foods there is.

Prep5 min
Cook40 min
MakesAbout 2 cups (8 to 10 baby portions)

No honeybefore 12 months, not even baked in or on a pacifier. It can cause infant botulism.

Ingredients

  • 1 medium sweet potato (about 10 oz)
  • Breast milk, prepared infant formula or water, to thin

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 400°F (200°C). Wash the sweet potato and prick it all over with a fork.
  2. Bake on a lined tray for 40 to 50 minutes, until a knife slides into the middle with no resistance.
  3. Let it cool, then split it open and scoop the flesh into a blender or bowl. Discard the skin.
  4. Blend or mash until smooth, adding liquid a little at a time until it drops easily off a spoon.

Texture for 6 months+

Completely smooth and spoonable, like thick yogurt. Thin with breast milk, formula or water a teaspoon at a time. As baby gets used to it, mash with a fork instead of blending to make it slightly thicker.

Choking check

  • Blend until there are no fibrous strands or firm pieces left.
  • Remove every bit of skin: it doesn't break down and can stick in baby's mouth.

Always seat baby upright and stay with them while they eat.

Allergens

None of the top 9 allergens. A gentle pick for days you're not introducing anything new.

Storage

Fridge up to 2 days in a sealed container, or freeze in an ice-cube tray, then bag the cubes, up to 1 month. Thaw in the fridge and warm gently. Stir and check the temperature before serving.

Tips

  • Steaming works too: peel, cube and steam for 12 to 15 minutes until very soft.
  • Mix a cube with lentil or meat puree later on for an iron boost.

Still under 12 months? Breast milk or infant formula is still your baby's main food. Solids add to milk, they don't replace it. Weaning from the breast before 12 months? Ask your pediatrician which infant formula to use.
