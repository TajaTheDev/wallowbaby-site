Yogurt and raspberry swirl

Plain whole-milk yogurt with mashed raspberries: a two-minute way to introduce dairy, no added sugar needed.

Prep3 min
Cook0 min
Makes1 baby portion

No honeybefore 12 months, not even baked in or on a pacifier. It can cause infant botulism.

Ingredients

  • 3 tbsp plain whole-milk yogurt (pasteurized, unsweetened)
  • 4 to 5 ripe raspberries

Method

  1. Mash the raspberries with a fork, then press them through a fine sieve to remove the seeds.
  2. Swirl the raspberry puree through the yogurt.
  3. Offer on a spoon. Serve cold or at room temperature.

Texture for 6 months+

Smooth, with the raspberries mashed and pressed through a sieve so there are no seeds or lumps for first tastes.

Choking check

  • Mash the raspberries fully. Sieving out the seeds keeps the texture smooth for new eaters.

Always seat baby upright and stay with them while they eat.

Allergens

  • Milk: Yogurt is a great first dairy food: cow's milk products like yogurt and cheese can be introduced before 12 months, even though cow's milk as a drink waits until 12 months.

New allergen? Offer it at home, earlier in the day, one new allergen at a time, then watch your baby for 2 hours. Hives, vomiting, swelling of the face or lips, or trouble breathing: call 911.

Storage

Best mixed fresh. Plain yogurt keeps in the fridge until its use-by date once opened, usually about a week.

Tips

  • Choose plain, unsweetened, whole-milk yogurt. Flavored and 'kids' yogurts often have a lot of added sugar.
  • Swap raspberries for mashed ripe banana, peach or mango.

Still under 12 months? Breast milk or infant formula is still your baby's main food. Solids add to milk, they don't replace it. Weaning from the breast before 12 months? Ask your pediatrician which infant formula to use.
