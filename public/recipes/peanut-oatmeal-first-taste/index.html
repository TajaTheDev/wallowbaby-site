Peanut first-taste oatmeal

A gentle way to introduce peanut: smooth peanut butter thinned and stirred into warm infant oat cereal.

Prep5 min
Cook2 min
Makes1 baby portion

No honeybefore 12 months, not even baked in or on a pacifier. It can cause infant botulism.

Ingredients

  • 2 tsp smooth peanut butter (just peanuts, no added sugar or salt)
  • 2 to 3 tsp warm water
  • 2 tbsp iron-fortified infant oat cereal
  • 3 to 4 tbsp breast milk or prepared infant formula

Method

  1. In a small bowl, stir the peanut butter with the warm water until it's completely smooth and runny.
  2. In another bowl, mix the infant cereal with the breast milk or formula.
  3. Stir 1 to 2 teaspoons of the thinned peanut butter into the cereal until no streaks remain.
  4. For a first try, offer a small taste on the tip of a spoon, wait about 10 minutes, then offer the rest if all is well.

Texture for 6 months+

Smooth and runny with no streaks or blobs of peanut butter left. It should drip off the spoon.

Choking check

  • Never give peanut butter straight off the spoon or in a thick blob: it can block a baby's airway. Always thin it.
  • Never give whole or chopped peanuts to babies or toddlers.

Always seat baby upright and stay with them while they eat.

Allergens

  • Peanut: If your baby has severe eczema or an egg allergy, they're at higher risk of peanut allergy: talk to your pediatrician before the first taste, as they may want allergy testing first. For other babies, the AAP supports introducing peanut early. Once it's tolerated, keep offering peanut regularly, a few times a week.

New allergen? Offer it at home, earlier in the day, one new allergen at a time, then watch your baby for 2 hours. Hives, vomiting, swelling of the face or lips, or trouble breathing: call 911.

Storage

Make fresh each time. Thinned peanut butter can be covered and kept in the fridge for 2 days.

Tips

  • Pick a time when baby is well, at home, earlier in the day, so you can watch them for 2 hours afterwards.
  • Peanut powder (just ground roasted peanuts) mixed into puree is another easy option.

Still under 12 months? Breast milk or infant formula is still your baby's main food. Solids add to milk, they don't replace it. Weaning from the breast before 12 months? Ask your pediatrician which infant formula to use.
