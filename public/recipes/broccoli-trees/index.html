Soft steamed broccoli trees

Big, soft broccoli florets with a built-in handle: an easy first finger food for little fists.

Prep5 min
Cook8 min
MakesAbout 6 florets

No honeybefore 12 months, not even baked in or on a pacifier. It can cause infant botulism.

Ingredients

  • 1 small head broccoli, cut into large florets with long stalks
  • 1 tsp olive oil or a little unsalted butter (optional)

Method

  1. Steam the florets for 7 to 10 minutes, until very tender and easily pierced through the stalk.
  2. Test one: press the head between your thumb and finger. If it doesn't squash easily, steam a few minutes more.
  3. Toss with a little olive oil or melted butter if you like, then cool before serving.

Texture for 8 months+

Very soft: the head should squash flat when you press it between your thumb and finger. Leave a long stalk as a handle.

Choking check

  • Serve large florets, not small pieces: a long stalk is easier for baby to hold and gnaw.
  • Undercooked broccoli is too firm. It must squash easily.

Always seat baby upright and stay with them while they eat.

Allergens

  • Milk (if using butter): Leave out the butter or use olive oil if your baby hasn't had dairy yet.

New allergen? Offer it at home, earlier in the day, one new allergen at a time, then watch your baby for 2 hours. Hives, vomiting, swelling of the face or lips, or trouble breathing: call 911.

Storage

Fridge up to 2 days. Reheat by steaming briefly, then cool. Freezing makes them soggy but fine for mashing.

Tips

  • Cauliflower and green beans (very soft, whole) work the same way.
  • Expect some broccoli to end up everywhere but baby's mouth. Playing with food is part of learning.

Still under 12 months? Breast milk or infant formula is still your baby's main food. Solids add to milk, they don't replace it. Weaning from the breast before 12 months? Ask your pediatrician which infant formula to use.
