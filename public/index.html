How it works

A bitter taste, a gentle nudge

Babies love sweet and dislike bitter. Wallow uses that simple fact to help make nursing less interesting, so other comforts can take its place.

1

Bitter melon

The serum is made with bitter melon, a vegetable eaten across Asia, Africa and the Caribbean that's famous for one thing: it's really, really bitter.

2

An unexpected taste

That bitterness makes nursing less appealing, which can make it easier to drop feeds and offer a cup, a cuddle or a snack instead.

3

Part of your plan

Works best alongside gradual weaning: drop one feed at a time, keep routines cozy, and give your body time to adjust.

What each ingredient does

Weaning, gently

A few things that help

Go one feed at a time

Drop a single feed every few days (often the midday one first). Gradual weaning is gentler on your baby and on your breasts.

Swap, don't just stop

Replace the feed with something else: a snack, a cup, a book, a walk. Under 12 months, that "something" includes infant formula, since milk is still baby's main food.

Listen to your body

Hard, hot, painful or red areas on your breasts, or a fever, can mean mastitis. Call your doctor, and ask a lactation consultant for a weaning plan that suits you.

Quick questions

What is Wallow Baby Weaner Serum?

A bitter-tasting topical made with bitter melon (Momordica charantia), distilled water and vegetable glycerin, in a 0.5 oz dropper bottle. Its bitter taste is designed to make nursing less appealing while you wean.

Does it change my milk supply?

No. It works by taste only and doesn't change how much milk you make. Your supply comes down naturally as you drop feeds. Weaning gradually helps avoid engorgement.
