Pair it with a gradual plan

  1. Pick the first feed to drop. Many families start with a midday feed. Keep the bedtime and first-morning feeds for last.
  2. Have a swap ready. A snack, a cup, a song, a walk outside. Under 12 months, swap in infant formula so baby still gets their milk.
  3. Wait a few days before dropping the next feed, so your body can adjust and your breasts don't get overfull.
  4. Lean into comfort. Extra cuddles, a favorite lovey, and a predictable routine help a lot.

Stop and check in if…

  • Your skin gets red, itchy or irritated after use: stop using and wash the area with water.
  • Your breasts become hard, hot, red or painful, or you get a fever: call your doctor (this can be mastitis).
  • Your baby seems unwell after nursing (vomiting, unusual sleepiness, floppiness or a rash): call your pediatrician, or Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222. In an emergency, call 911.
